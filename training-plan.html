10K Build, then Timbuk 22K · Oct 5, 2026 – Feb 21, 2027

Training Plan

Overview

Weekly training by day, prescribed in minutes. Running days are Monday, Wednesday, Thursday and Saturday; strength days are Tuesday and Friday; Sunday is rest. The goal for the fall is to complete the Haskins Hustle 5K and the Johnstown Turkey Trot 10K safely, without overextending. Weeks 1–7 follow a run/walk ladder modeled on the Beginner Runner Program: run intervals lengthen from 2 minutes to 3, 5, 8, 10, 12 and 15, then stack into longer blocks (20 + 20 + 10, 30 + 15, 40 + 15, 45 + 10) until 60 minutes continuous on Saturday, Nov 14. Monday and Wednesday move the ladder forward; Thursday is active recovery that repeats an earlier step; Saturday holds the week’s milestone. Everything is easy effort. The 5K is a halfway checkpoint and the 10K closes the block, with Week 7 as a short taper. The minutes shown include the walk breaks; the walk warm-up and cool-down don’t count. If a workout feels like a struggle, repeat it before moving on: the race dates are fixed, and everything else can slide. Five check-ins along the way decide whether to continue or adjust. Tap any shaded cell for the session details.

After the 10K, Week 8 is a recovery week, then a 12-week build to the Timbuk 22K, the A race. The point of Timbuk is covering the distance at a steady effort, not speed. It’s two loops of a hilly 11K course in Granville, on a Sunday in February. Expect about 2:50–3:15 on your feet (around 3:00 if the course is dry and the winter goes well), so the winter block is about time on feet and climbing strength. The hill repeats build strength for the course’s climbs, not speed. Weeks 9–14 are easy base running with long runs moving onto hilly trails; hill repeats start on Wednesdays in Week 15 only if you pass the readiness check in the grid notes. Long runs grow 10 minutes at a time to a 130-minute race rehearsal on Feb 6, with down weeks at Christmas and mid-January. Weeks 16–18 (240–255 minutes) are above your biggest week ever; if you’ve missed a week or feel flat by mid-January, cap the long run at 120 minutes and the peak week at about 230.

  • Run/walk ladder (Weeks 1–7): “12 × (2 run / 1 walk)” means 2 minutes running, 1 minute walking, 12 times. Every session starts and ends with a walk: 5 minutes in Weeks 1–2, 4 in Weeks 3–4, 3 in Weeks 5–7. All running is easy effort. If a step feels like a struggle, repeat it before moving on.
  • Wednesday strides (Weeks 8–20): in the last 10 minutes, run 4–6 strides of 20 seconds, quick and relaxed, with a full walk-back recovery. Everything else is easy.
  • Thursday: it follows Wednesday’s run, so keep it under about 140 HR. In Weeks 1–7 it repeats an earlier step of the ladder. It’s the first run to cut if your legs feel heavy.
  • After a race or a hard Saturday: Sunday is rest and Monday stays short and easy.
  • Warning signs: easy-run HR creeping up, cadence well below your usual 147–150, or cutting a run short. Any of these means repeating the current week instead of moving on, and swapping that week’s hard session for an easy run.
  • Squeezed week? The minimum is two 20-minute easy runs. A week never drops to zero.
  • Ready for hills (Week 15)? Only if you’ve had six or more weeks in a row with no missed week and easy runs staying under about 140 HR. If not, run those Wednesdays all easy; the rest of the plan works the same.
  • Long runs of 90 minutes or more: practice fueling. Take something at 30–45 minutes and every 30–45 minutes after, so race day uses what you’ve tested.
  • Missed a run? Skip it rather than doubling up. Lost a week to illness or travel? Repeat the previous week’s long run before moving on.

Strength Plan

Tuesday is lower body, alternating squat weeks and deadlift weeks, plus rows, overhead press and carries. Friday is upper body only, so nothing loads your legs before Saturday’s long run. Keep 2–3 reps in reserve on every working set. Tap any strength cell in the grid for the full session.

Core Routine

The same lower-back and hip stability routine follows every strength session, and it doesn’t scale down in light weeks. On the Fridays before the 10K and the 22K, and on Christmas, when there’s no lifting, do the stretches only.
