16-Week Hybrid Strength + Half Marathon Build  ·  Aug 2 – Nov 15, 2026

Training Plan

Overview

Weekly mileage and lifting by day, building toward the Run to Feed the Hungry Half Marathon on Nov 14. This season's focus is building the aerobic base and durability for the distance while holding onto strength — speed work is deferred to next season, once that base is in place. Monday and Wednesday pair a strength session in the morning with an easy run in the evening; Tuesday is cross-training (an easy bike, swim, or elliptical session), 30 minutes, dropping to 20 in the taper and skipped entirely in race week; Thursday is an easy run with strides worked in near the end; Friday is upper-body strength only, with an optional easy cross-training session (bike, swim, or elliptical) alongside it, and no run, to protect Saturday's long run; Saturday carries the long run, progressing on a four-week rhythm with a cutback every fourth week; Sunday is rest. Weekly mileage is set from Saturday's long run, and Monday/Wednesday/Thursday split that total by formula — so the whole week's volume scales consistently off the long run driving it.

Weekly mileage is derived from Saturday's long run: round(long run ÷ 0.45) — so the long run stays roughly 45% of the week, consistent with typical long-run-to-weekly-volume ratios. Week 14 is a fixed exception at 24 miles rather than the ~27 the formula would otherwise give, capping peak-week volume intentionally. From that weekly total, Monday and Thursday take fixed shares (ceiling(weekly × 0.15) and floor(weekly × 0.15) respectively) and Wednesday absorbs the remainder after Monday, Thursday, and Saturday are accounted for. Any run day whose formula result falls under 2 miles is set to a 2-mile floor. Race week (16) sits outside this formula entirely, since Saturday that week is the race itself.

Easy Running & Strides

No quality (tempo or interval) sessions this cycle — every run this season is easy effort, aside from short strides. That's a deliberate trade: quality work is what sharpens pace, but it's also where most running injuries happen and where recovery gets borrowed from the lifting program. With a regular running base but no half-marathon-distance experience yet, this season prioritizes durability and aerobic volume over a faster ceiling. Quality can be added next season once that base is established.

Thursday's easy run includes 4–6 × 15–20 second relaxed strides near the end, with a full recovery walk between each. This is about turnover and running economy, not effort — it is not a quality session, and it carries almost none of the fatigue cost. Strides drop out in the final taper and race week, when the priority shifts entirely to arriving fresh.

Strength Program

Three sessions per week — Monday, Wednesday, and Friday — with a fixed exercise list that stays the same for the whole plan; only sets, reps, and volume change by phase. Monday is full body, heavy, barbell-based, and paired with that day's easy run: back squat, deadlift, a bench-or-shoulder press (alternating weekly), and a row — this is the day with the most recovery runway before Saturday's long run. Wednesday is lower body, moderate, dumbbell/band-based, also paired with that day's easy run: a squat variation (Bulgarian split or goblet), single-leg RDL, lunges or step-ups, and calf raises. Friday is upper body only, moderate, dumbbell/band-based, with zero leg loading: DB bench press, DB shoulder press, DB skull crushers, DB curls, and banded rows — sitting the day before Saturday's long run, since lower-body soreness peaks 24–48 hours after a session, which would otherwise land squarely on the long run. Every session closes with two rotating core exercises — one from Glute Activation, one from Stability — with push-ups occasionally worked in on Monday or Friday for extra pressing volume. Fixed exercise list, explicit each week: Monday, Wednesday, and Friday keep the same exercise categories for the entire 16 weeks — only sets, reps, and load change week to week. Monday is back squat, deadlift, press, and row; Wednesday is a squat variation, single-leg RDL, lunges/step-ups, and calf raises; Friday is DB bench press, DB shoulder press, DB skull crushers, DB curls, and banded rows. Core rotates two exercises per session from a small library — Glute Activation (clamshells, lateral leg raise, hip thrust) and Stability (dead bug, heel taps, side plank) — plus push-ups as a pressing/anterior-core option.

Weeks 1–4 (Base): Full volume as programmed — this is where the heaviest squat and deadlift numbers get pushed while running mileage is still low.

Weeks 5–8 (Build): Intensity holds, but lower-body sets trim slightly as running mileage climbs — the first give comes from lifting volume, not from the run, since mileage is what builds the distance-specific fitness this season is for.

Weeks 9–12 (Peak Build): Lower body trims further on both Monday and Wednesday. Upper body (Friday) stays unchanged — it doesn't interfere with running legs, so there's no need to pull it back early. Glute and stability core work stays in every session regardless of how much the main lifts trim — it's cheap insurance against running-related hip and knee issues as mileage rises.

Weeks 13–14 (Peak): As the long run peaks at 11–12 miles, lower body drops to a maintenance dose on both Monday and Wednesday — same exercises, held load, minimal sets, goal is holding onto what's been built rather than progressing further. Upper body remains unchanged.

Week 15 (Taper): One light pass through the full list on all three days, volume cut 30–50% — just enough to feel normal, nothing more.

Week 16 (Race): Strength drops out entirely. The race is the only output this week — that's the real freshness buffer, not the taper before it.
