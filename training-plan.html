18-Week Half Marathon Build  ·  Jul 19 – Nov 15, 2026

Training Plan

Overview

Weekly mileage by day, building toward the Run to Feed the Hungry Half Marathon on Nov 14. This season's focus is distance and endurance, not speed — speed can be next season's project. Quality follows a 15-mile weekly floor (weeks under that run strides only) and targets half marathon race pace, growing from a quarter mile to a full mile so that pace feels familiar by race day.

Quality

Every quality rep targets half marathon race pace, not a faster or slower effort. Training for one specific race means the most valuable use of a limited number of hard sessions is rehearsing the exact effort race day demands, so it's a known feeling by Nov 14 rather than something guessed at cold. That's a departure from a polarized model (easy running plus genuinely hard VO2max work) — this plan spends its quality time in the moderate zone on purpose, because moderate effort *is* race effort here. It also matches this season's goal: distance and endurance, not speed. The volume progression builds that; quality reinforces it rather than chasing a faster ceiling. Speed can be next season's focus, once a bigger endurance base is in place.

The distance ladder — quarter mile, then half mile, up to a full mile at Peak — reflects that holding race pace for 30 seconds and for 8+ minutes are different skills at the same pace, so reps build at each distance before stepping up. Deload weeks hold the current distance rather than pushing further, consolidating rather than adding fatigue. The hard part of any session stays under a fifth of that week's total mileage — the same reason weeks under 15 miles skip quality for strides. Once a week clears that floor, quality kicks in and stays for the rest of the build. Taper reverts to the shortest version, keeping the pace familiar without adding late fatigue. Race weeks skip the structure entirely — the race itself is the ultimate rehearsal.

Strength Program

Three sessions per week — Tuesday, Thursday, and Friday — with a fixed exercise list that stays the same for the whole plan — only sets, reps, and load change by phase, and every week spells out exactly which press variation to use, so there's nothing to track. Tuesday is heavy full-body: squat, Romanian deadlift, a press, and lunges, plus a core/hip finisher. Thursday fills in what Tuesday and Friday don't cover — a row, a light leg accessory movement, and calf work, plus the same core/hip finisher — deliberately with no heavy pressing, since Tuesday and Friday already handle that. Friday is upper body: a press, curls, banded rows, and banded lateral raises, plus the same core/hip finisher. Fixed exercise list, explicit each week: Tuesday, Thursday, and Friday keep the same exercise categories for the entire 18 weeks — Tuesday is squat, Romanian deadlift, a press, lunges, and the core/hip finisher (lateral leg raise, clamshell, dead bug, push-up); Thursday is a row, a light leg accessory movement, calf raises, and the same core/hip finisher; Friday is a press, DB curl, banded row, banded lateral raise, and the same core/hip finisher. The press variation (shoulder or bench) is called out explicitly every single week rather than left to "alternate" — Tuesday and Friday always run opposite choices in the same week, so both movements get even volume and there's nothing to remember. Only sets, reps, and load change week to week — the exercises themselves never get swapped out.

Weeks 1–11 (Foundation/Build): Working sets and progressive load, but only one of squat or Romanian deadlift progresses on any given Tuesday — the other holds at a steady, controlled weight. Squat-emphasis and deadlift-emphasis weeks alternate, so you never add load to both lower-body lifts in the same session. Thursday's leg accessory work stays light throughout — it's there for pull volume and calves, not to add lower-body load — and Friday stays zero leg loading, because Friday sits the day before Saturday's long run and lower-body soreness peaks 24–48 hours after a session — exactly the window that would otherwise land on the long run.

Week 12 exception: Tuesday carries a 3-mile run that week instead of a rest/lift day, so Tuesday's strength session is dropped entirely rather than stacked with the run — Thursday runs as usual, and Friday runs the same exercise list at reduced sets instead of two full sessions.

Weeks 13–16 (Peak): As running volume climbs to 22–26 mi/week, strength shifts to maintenance across all three days — same exercise list and working weight, fewer sets, squat and RDL both held steady rather than progressing, and Thursday trims to two sets across the board. The goal is holding onto what's been built, not progressing further, while running takes priority.

Week 17 (Taper) and Week 18 (Race): Taper week runs one light, single-set pass through the full Tuesday list just to feel normal, nothing more — no Thursday or Friday session, so legs and upper body both stay fresh. Race week skips strength entirely — the race is that week's only output.
