Reset, Turkey Trot, then Timbuk 22K · Oct 12, 2026 – Feb 21, 2027

Training Plan

Overview

This plan starts with a reset. Calf strain, shin splints and heel pain all point to lower legs that took on more than they were ready for: the first week of October roughly doubled your recent running and added new leg work at the same time. Your aerobic fitness is fine. Your calves, shins and feet need time to catch up. So for now it’s three strength days and spin on the bike instead of running, with the lifting aimed at the calves and feet.

Running comes back when you pass the return-to-run tests below, and not before two full weeks with no running. Weeks 1 and 2 are strength and spin only. The tests come on Monday Oct 26, so the earliest run is Tuesday Oct 27, and the grid assumes that date. Until you pass, every run in the grid becomes the spin session shown under it. Runs come back on Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday as run/walk, building a step at a time. The Haskins Hustle 5K comes only four days after your first run, so walk it (a few short jogs are fine) or skip it. The Johnstown Turkey Trot 10K is about finishing, with no time goal.

After a recovery week, the winter build to Timbuk uses three runs a week with running capped near 200 minutes. That ceiling sits just under your biggest week ever; every time this year you went past four runs and about 200 minutes, a slump followed. An optional easy spin on Friday supplies extra aerobic work without the pounding. Long runs grow 10 minutes at a time to a 130-minute race rehearsal on Feb 6. You decide between the 22K and the 11K at the Jan 9 check-in.

Return to Running

Take the tests on Monday, Oct 26, before lifting, after two full weeks with no running. If you don’t pass, repeat them every few days. Every test has to be pain-free, during and the next morning:

  • Walk: 30 minutes at a brisk pace.
  • Calf raises: 20–25 single-leg calf raises on each side, full range, steady tempo.
  • Hops: 10 single-leg hops on each side.
  • Morning: no heel pain on your first steps out of bed.

Pass all four, and your next scheduled run day is your first run/walk. If you’re cleared after Oct 27, start with Week 3’s first run on the day you’re cleared and move through the steps in order. Don’t skip steps to catch up. A late start just means more walking at the Turkey Trot.

Pain rule once you’re running: discomfort up to about 2 out of 10 that’s gone by the next morning is fine. Anything more, a limp, or pain that’s worse the next morning means swapping the next run for spin and repeating the previous step once it settles.

See a sports medicine doctor or PT promptly if squeezing the sides of your heel hurts, a pinpoint spot on the shin bone hurts to press, pain wakes you at night, or the heel isn’t clearly better by about Nov 1. Day to day, avoid going barefoot on hard floors, wear cushioned shoes (a heel cup may help), and check the mileage on your running shoes.

  • Weeks 1–2 (Oct 12–25): no running. In Week 1 lifting is Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday, spin is Wednesday and Friday, and Sunday is rest. From Oct 19 the weekly pattern is Lower A Monday, Lower B Wednesday, Upper Friday, with spin (later runs) Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday and rest on Sunday.
  • Run/walk ratios: “2:1” means 2 minutes running and 1 minute walking, repeated. Ratios are set through the Turkey Trot to control the load while your legs recover. From December, take walk breaks as suits the day. Every run starts and ends with a few minutes of walking, which isn’t counted.
  • Not cleared yet? Any run becomes the spin session shown under it.
  • Spin: stay seated and conversational, at a cadence of 85–95 rpm. Heart rate on the bike runs about 10 beats lower than running at the same effort, so easy is roughly 120–135. Set the saddle so your knee is slightly bent at the bottom of the stroke. While your calves are recovering, skip standing climbs at low cadence.
  • Optional Friday spin (from Week 3): 30–45 minutes easy after upper body. It stands in for a fourth run. Skip it if your legs feel heavy.
  • Thursday follows Wednesday’s Lower B, so keep it short and easy (under about 140 HR). It’s the first run to cut if your legs feel heavy.
  • Adding a fourth run: only after 4–6 weeks with no missed week and no next-morning pain. Make it 20–30 minutes easy on Monday before Lower A, and keep that week’s total running about the same. Total minutes matter more than the number of days.
  • Ready for hills (Week 14)? Only with six or more weeks in a row and no missed week, and easy runs staying under about 140 HR. If not, run those Tuesdays easy. Uphill power-hiking works as a substitute either way.
  • Long runs of 90 minutes or more: practice fueling. Take something at 30–45 minutes and every 30–45 minutes after.
  • Missed a run? Skip it rather than doubling up. Lost a week? Repeat the previous week’s long run before moving on. A squeezed week is at least two 20-minute easy runs, never zero.

Strength Plan

Lower A (Monday) is squat-based, with calf and shin work. Lower B (Wednesday) is deadlift plus single-leg work and calf raises. Upper (Friday) is upper body only, plus calf isometric holds that don’t tire your legs before Saturday. Squat and deadlift are on separate days now, which is easier to recover from. Keep 2–3 reps in reserve on every working set, and own the technique before adding weight. The old Monday/Thursday bodyweight leg routine is folded into these sessions. Tap any strength cell for the full session.

Calf and foot work is the priority. The seated (bent-knee) calf raise trains the soleus, the muscle that takes most of the load when you run. Weeks 1–2: bodyweight and isometric holds only. From Week 3, add load in small jumps as long as your heel and calf stay quiet. Stop any exercise that brings back calf or heel pain.
