18-Week Half Marathon Build  ·  Jul 19 – Nov 15, 2026

Training Plan

Overview

Weekly mileage by day, building toward the Run to Feed the Hungry Half Marathon on Nov 14. Quality follows a 15-mile weekly floor — any week under that runs strides only on Wednesday, including week 7, which clears 15 miles but sits right before a race week and a deload dip. Real quality begins in week 10 as oval turnover work: short, relaxed reps building from a quarter mile up to a full mile, aimed at light feet, quick turnover, and steady breathing rather than pace or lactate threshold.

Quality

Wednesday holds a 3-mile floor even on the lightest deload weeks, but the bigger rule is on weekly volume: any week under 15 miles total runs strides only. Week 7 clears 15 miles on its own, but the race week and deload dip that follow it (weeks 8-9) would cut off any real turnover work almost immediately, so it stays strides too — real quality starts in week 10, where it can run uninterrupted the rest of the way. From there, quality is oval turnover work: repeated reps run at a quick, light, relaxed turnover — not race pace, not lactate threshold. The distance builds steadily across the remaining quality weeks — quarter mile, then half mile, then three-quarter mile — topping out at full-mile repeats in Peak week, the longest rep of the build. Reps trim down as the distance grows to keep total time on the oval reasonable, and deload weeks step the distance back rather than holding the build's current length. Taper closes back out at the short, easy quarter-mile version the progression opened with — enough to keep the legs feeling quick without adding fatigue before race day. The cue throughout is light ground contact and steady breathing, never a push for pace — if a rep starts to feel like a hard sustained effort rather than a relaxed one, that's the signal to ease off. The hard part of any session stays under a fifth of that week's total mileage; the rest of the day is easy running. Race weeks override the structure with an easy run + strides — the race is that week's quality session. Paces are derived from a 9:25/mi 5K (Jun 3) and a ~12:00/mi easy baseline.

Strength Program

Two sessions per week, Tuesday and Friday, with a fixed exercise list that stays the same for the whole plan — only sets, reps, and load change by phase, and every week spells out exactly which press variation to use, so there's nothing to track. Tuesday is full-body: squat, Romanian deadlift, a press, and lunges, plus a core/hip finisher. Friday is upper body: a press, curls, banded rows, and banded lateral raises, plus the same core/hip finisher. Fixed exercise list, explicit each week: Tuesday and Friday keep the same exercise categories for the entire 18 weeks — Tuesday is squat, Romanian deadlift, a press, lunges, and the core/hip finisher (lateral leg raise, clamshell, dead bug, push-up); Friday is a press, DB curl, banded row, banded lateral raise, and the same core/hip finisher. The press variation (shoulder or bench) is called out explicitly every single week rather than left to "alternate" — Tuesday and Friday always run opposite choices in the same week, so both movements get even volume and there's nothing to remember. Only sets, reps, and load change week to week — the exercises themselves never get swapped out.

Weeks 1–11 (Foundation/Build): Working sets and progressive load, but only one of squat or Romanian deadlift progresses on any given Tuesday — the other holds at a steady, controlled weight. Squat-emphasis and deadlift-emphasis weeks alternate, so you never add load to both lower-body lifts in the same session. Friday stays zero leg loading, because Friday sits the day before Saturday's long run and lower-body soreness peaks 24–48 hours after a session — exactly the window that would otherwise land on the long run.

Week 12 exception: Tuesday carries a 3-mile run that week instead of a rest/lift day, so Tuesday's strength session is dropped entirely rather than stacked with the run — Friday runs the same exercise list at reduced sets instead of two full sessions.

Weeks 13–16 (Peak): As running volume climbs to 22–26 mi/week, strength shifts to maintenance — same exercise list and working weight, fewer sets, squat and RDL both held steady rather than progressing. The goal is holding onto what's been built, not progressing further, while running takes priority.

Week 17 (Taper) and Week 18 (Race): Taper week runs one light, single-set pass through the full Tuesday list just to feel normal, nothing more — no Friday session, so legs and upper body both stay fresh. Race week skips strength entirely — the race is that week's only output.
